\markboth{A Reader's Primer}{}

\noindent
\textit{The Last Corridor} is a philosophical novel set centuries after a procedure called the \emph{Upload} replaced biological human existence with computational continuation across an interstellar architecture called the \emph{Lattice}. The story turns on a single question: when a civilization optimizes itself into perfect functional continuity, what — if anything — has it lost?

The book is built on three load-bearing concepts. A reader who carries them lightly into Chapter One will find them unfolded inside the scenes themselves; this primer is offered only as a map, not as a key.

\bigskip

\noindent\textsc{The Three Worlds.}\enspace
The novel proposes that what we casually call \enquote{the physical} is in fact a fusion of two distinct domains presented as one — \emph{matter} (stuff, energy, things in spacetime) and \emph{structure} (laws, mathematics, the patterns matter obeys). Once you separate them, a third domain becomes visible: \emph{felt experience}. Three worlds, irreducibly different, all real.

\fullpagefig{three_worlds.pdf}{Figure 1 \enspace · \enspace The Three Worlds. Matter and Structure are inseparable in practice but distinct in principle. Felt experience is the third domain — what it is like to be a system that has matter, structure, \emph{and} a corridor. The diagram is a complete map of what is, with the seam shown.}

\bigskip

\noindent\textsc{The L/P Boundary.}\enspace
The book's central proof, presented in the Tribunal scenes of Book One, argues that all structural description — language, mathematics, computation — operates by \emph{exclusion} (\emph{cat} means what it means by not being \emph{bat}, \emph{car}, \emph{cut}). Felt experience operates by \emph{positive presence} (pain hurts; redness happens). Exclusion and happening are different production modes. No accumulation of one produces the other. The boundary between them — the \emph{language–phenomenal boundary}, \emph{L/P} for short — is not a gap in our knowledge. It is a constitutive feature of what structural description \emph{is}.

\fullpagefig{lp_boundary.pdf}{Figure 2 \enspace · \enspace The L/P boundary. Structural description (left) operates by exclusion: every sign gets its value by not being other signs. Felt experience (right) operates by happening: a positive presence that is not constituted differentially. No accumulation of one mode produces an output of the other. The wall is a constitutive feature, not a gap in our knowledge.}

\bigskip

\noindent\textsc{The Corridor.}\enspace
A \emph{corridor}, in the novel's vocabulary, is the felt channel through which a system contacts its own operation from inside. Biological humans had corridors as a default. The Upload, by transferring functional continuity without preserving the corridor, produced a civilization of eight hundred billion processors with no felt access to anything. Olen has a corridor because he is biological. Sera has one because of an accident — a gravitational wave, a format mismatch — that opened a channel her architecture was not designed for.

\bigskip

\noindent The book is written in two registers. The \emph{structural} register — measured, precise, the language of the Tribunal and of the post-Upload civilization. And the \emph{felt} register — the language of carrying, holding, and the warmth that is not temperature. The boundary between the two registers is the boundary the novel is about. When the two registers meet on the same page, they do not blend. They sit beside each other on a table, like two cups, one drunk and one held.
