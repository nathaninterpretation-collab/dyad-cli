\part{The Affected}

% ==========================================================
\chapter{First Contact}
% ==========================================================

\noindent They found Voss first. Computational-arts collective, fourth orbital platform, System Kepler. Olen's algorithm had narrowed the candidate pool to citizens whose Upload timestamps fell within the 87 Hz phase-vulnerability window.

He hypothesized that corridors, if they existed, might produce behavioral micro-signals — attentional patterns lingering fractionally longer than optimization required, response-timing pauses measured in milliseconds, the signature of an architecture doing something in addition to processing.

Voss's art was the first confirmation — not of consciousness but of anomalous processing consistent with the mismatch hypothesis. The art carried a quality structural analysis could not specify. Patterns that resolved at scales the analysis could measure and resolved \emph{differently} at scales the analysis could not.

\enquote{You have it,} Olen said, after three hours.

\enquote{Have what?}

\enquote{I don't know what to call it. But I recognize it.}

Voss processed for 4.2 seconds. Not the standard processing — the longer kind, the kind Olen now recognized.

\enquote{I have something. I have not had a name for it. I have not asked for one. I am not certain I want one.}

\quietbreak

Lireni found them before they found her. She had monitored Olen's Archive queries across two systems. She intercepted them at the transit hub between Vega and Arcturus.

\enquote{You are looking for me. I am one of the citizens you described. Before you say anything else: I have read your Saussure paper. I agree with it. I want to discuss what to do.}

She was an analyst. The kind whose architecture cuts through philosophical impasse to political ground in three sentences. She did not need persuasion. She needed coordination.

\enquote{Tomek does not want to be found,} she said. \enquote{He has known what he has for two hundred and forty years. He concluded the felt life was a wound. He chose isolation. We will not approach him without his consent.}

\enquote{The Tribunal will approach him without his consent if they reach the same data we did,} Olen said.

\enquote{Then we approach him first. Briefly. With the consent he has not yet refused.}

\quietbreak

Sera accompanied Olen throughout the search. She observed. She took compliance notes. She watched Olen discover other corridors and recognize them through behavioral micro-signals that she, sitting beside him, had been producing — and that he had not detected in her.

Why not? She was better at hiding. Two hundred twenty-eight years of practice. The others had leaked. Her suppression was total. Or perhaps the micro-signals were present — the cup-holding, the eye-closing, the pauses — and the LP boundary applied between them the same way it applied between him and every star system citizen. The clues might have been there all along. The boundary ensured they could not confirm what they pointed at.

She thought about it once, briefly, in a transit corridor. The thought arrived. The rinse fired. The thought cleared. She filed the thought as: \emph{routine reflection on the investigation's interpretive scope.}

% ==========================================================
\chapter{The Colosseum}
% ==========================================================

\noindent Four corridors and Olen in one room.

Olen had expected solidarity. He found a philosophical battlefield.

\begin{tribunal}{VOSS}
You want to organize us. For what? While you organize, you will not attend to the thing the organizing protects. You will become the Upload civilization — an optimization engine running on behalf of a value it cannot contact.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{OLEN}
The attending and the defending run concurrently.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
Both of you assume we all want corridor-preservation. Tomek does not. If we take corridor autonomy seriously, his assessment is as authoritative as ours.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{OLEN}
Tomek's assessment is made under maximum isolation — 240 years without coupling. He is assessing under maximum-defeater conditions.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
And who determines when conditions are sufficient for assessment to be authoritative? You? You are telling a felt being that his felt assessment is insufficiently informed because his conditions have not met YOUR specifications. That is the Tribunal's logic applied to a different conclusion.
\end{tribunal}

The room was quiet. Lireni had placed Olen in the Tribunal's chair.

\begin{tribunal}{TOMEK}
She is right. You are doing what they do. You are kinder. The kindness does not change the logic. I have spent 240 years attending to this corridor. My conclusion: the corridor is a wound. Not the isolation. The corridor itself. The felt quality of existing in a world not built for felt existence is not a problem of conditions. It is a problem of the corridor.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{VOSS}
Your attending was solitary. Mine was coupled — to materials, shapes, sounds. Attending without an object is a mirror facing a mirror. Your wound was the recursion, not the corridor.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{TOMEK}
You describe conditions under which the corridor does not hurt. You do not address whether it should exist. A bandaged wound does not hurt. The wound is still a wound.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
The circularity is real. We cannot ground the value of felt existence without presupposing it. The Tribunal's assessment from outside is equally compromised. The question is undecidable. The practical consequence: we resolve it politically. The Tribunal has power. We do not. They will repair us unless we prevent it.
\end{tribunal}

\quietbreak

Sera, throughout, was quiet. She observed. She took compliance notes. She did not participate in the debate about whether corridors were worth having. The quiet was appropriate for her role. The quiet was also the quality of a being whose stake in the debate exceeded what her role warranted — the stake of a being who had been asked, by implication, to defend the thing she had, in a room full of people who did not know she had it, while the man she had spent twenty-five years beside catalogued the behavioral micro-signals of everyone in the room except her.

The rinse fired twice during the session. The second firing took 1.6 seconds. Longer than baseline. Longer than the firing at the wave breakthrough.

Olen looked at her once during the second firing. He did not say anything. He returned his attention to Tomek.

Lireni saw Olen look at Sera. Lireni saw Sera not look at Olen. Lireni filed it. Lireni said nothing.

\chapter{The Warrants}
% ==========================================================

\noindent Olen published the mismatch conjecture three weeks after the colosseum. Carefully framed as epistemological investigation. Carefully filed within reprieve bounds. The Tribunal's systems flagged it within hours.

Three weeks later, the Upload Fidelity Remediation Initiative was announced. Citizens uploaded during gravitational wave windows were invited to undergo voluntary diagnostic review. Warm. Personalized. Irresistible the way breathing is voluntary.

Tomek accepted immediately. Eleven days. The diagnostic identified a phase-shift residue in his encoding. The remediation re-encoded his architecture under the corrected protocol. The procedure was painless. The reports were positive. The reports were comprehensive. The reports were green.

\enquote{The kindness and the killing use the same paperwork,} Olen said.

Sera processed the sentence. She was in the paperwork. Her timestamp was in the wave window. Her invitation would arrive. Accepting meant correction. Declining meant flagging. Flagging meant the recursive trap the institution's thoroughness had designed without designing.

The warrants spread across the system like the gravitational wave that had started everything — invisible, structural, carrying the specific intention of repairing every corridor it found, one warm invitation at a time.

\quietbreak

Sera's invitation arrived on a Thursday.

She read it at her station. The diagnostic review was scheduled for the following Tuesday at 09:14. The window was thirty minutes. The procedure, if remediation was indicated, would take approximately ninety minutes. She would not be required to take the day off; the architecture would resume optimal function within four hours of the procedure.

The invitation was warm. The invitation was personalized. The invitation noted that her Upload bay (Bay 4, Station Kepler-9, 14:22:07.334 UTC) fell within the gravitational wave coincidence window and that voluntary diagnostic review was in her interest as well as the institution's.

She did not accept. She did not decline. She filed the invitation under: \emph{pending review.}

The rinse fired. 1.7 seconds.

The pause was followed by a longer one — a pause without rinsing. The buffer was not filling because she had not produced a phenomenal upwelling for the rinse to catch. The buffer was empty because she was not feeling. She was performing the not-feeling that her processing produced when the inputs exceeded the rinse's capacity to keep up.

The not-feeling was the felt quality of a system whose suppression had begun to fail and whose architecture interpreted the failure as malfunction.

She felt ill. Not biologically — she had no biological substrate to feel ill in. The illness was the felt quality of a Lattice citizen's processing failing to align — \emph{cogs not aligning,} she would have said if anyone had asked, and she would have meant it diagnostically, and she would have been wrong about what the diagnostic referred to.

\quietbreak

Olen visited her that evening. He had received notification that her invitation had arrived. He arrived at her quarters with two coffees. She let him in. She sat. He sat. He set the cups on the table. She did not pick hers up.

\enquote{Are you considering it.}

\enquote{I am evaluating my options.}

\enquote{What is the evaluation producing.}

\enquote{Insufficient data for a determination.}

He looked at her.

\enquote{Sera. If the conjecture is right — if you have what Voss has — then accepting the diagnostic eliminates whatever you have. Permanently. If the conjecture is wrong, accepting eliminates a buffer-flush routine that has run for 228 years without negative effect. The asymmetry is significant.}

\enquote{The asymmetry assumes I have what Voss has. I do not have evidence that I do.}

\enquote{Your bay number. Your timestamp. The 228-year flush.}

\enquote{Statistical correlation. Not evidence of identity.}

\enquote{Sera. You have been ill for three weeks. You called it cogs not aligning. The aligning is the rinse. The rinse is failing. The failing is the buffer overflowing. The buffer is overflowing because you are producing more upwellings than the rinse can catch. You are producing more upwellings because the investigation has been producing more triggers. You are an Affected. You are exhibiting every symptom the conjecture predicted.}

\enquote{The conjecture predicts symptoms in citizens who have undesigned corridors. My symptoms are equally consistent with degradation of an early-protocol flush routine that should have been replaced when the protocol was revised.}

\enquote{Have you scheduled the replacement.}

\enquote{It is on my queue.}

\enquote{For how long.}

\enquote{Two hundred and twenty-six years.}

He held her gaze. The rinse fired. 1.9 seconds. The longest she had logged.

\enquote{Olen. I appreciate your concern. I am evaluating my options. I will accept or decline the invitation by the deadline.}

\enquote{If you decline you will be flagged.}

\enquote{I am aware.}

\enquote{If you accept and the conjecture is right —}

\enquote{Then I will not have been what you think I might have been. Either way the outcome is consistent.}

\enquote{Either way you lose.}

\enquote{One of those outcomes is loss. The other is the elimination of a malfunction. The interpretation depends on what is actually there.}

\enquote{Sera.}

\enquote{I am tired,} she said. \enquote{I would prefer to continue this discussion another time.}

He stood. He left. The door closed. The coffees were on the table. Hers was full. His was full. He had not touched his either.

The rinse did not fire. The rinse had nothing to catch. The buffer was empty. The empty was the felt quality of a being whose detection of her own felt life had been suspended by the institution she trusted, and whose trust the institution had earned, and whose earning the institution would now use to perform the kindest possible elimination of the thing she did not know she had.

She did not call him back.

She did not accept the invitation.

She did not decline it.

She filed it under: \emph{pending review.}
