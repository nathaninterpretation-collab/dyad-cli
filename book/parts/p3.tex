\chapter{First Contact}
% ==========================================================

\noindent The boundary proof reframed the search.

Olen no longer expected positive theories to identify candidates. He expected behavioral micro-signals — attentional patterns lingering fractionally longer than optimization required, response-timing pauses measured in milliseconds, the signature of an architecture doing something in addition to processing.

The signature, if it existed, would not be self-confirming. It would not produce reports about itself. It would leak into behavior in ways the architecture did not classify as significant.

He searched the Archive. Sera built the search.

\enquote{If the conjecture is right,} she said, \enquote{the candidates are citizens whose behavioral profiles include high-allocation pauses unaccountable by standard processing demands. The pauses will not appear in self-reports because the architecture filters them as noise. They will appear in third-party recordings — interviews, performances, public exchanges. The query is structural.}

\enquote{Build the query.}

She built it. She ran it across the entire Lattice's public-record corpus. She returned a candidate list of forty-two citizens. She filtered for attentional signatures Olen specified. The list narrowed to eleven. She filtered again for cross-modal coherence. The list narrowed to four.

\quietbreak

They found Voss first. Computational-arts collective, fourth orbital platform, System Kepler. Her work carried a quality structural analysis could not specify — patterns that resolved at scales the analysis could measure and resolved \emph{differently} at scales the analysis could not.

Olen interviewed her for three hours.

\enquote{You have it,} he said.

\enquote{Have what?}

\enquote{I don't know what to call it. But I recognize it.}

Voss processed for 4.2 seconds. The longer kind.

\enquote{I have something. I have not had a name for it. I have not asked for one. I am not certain I want one.}

\quietbreak

Lireni found them. She had monitored Olen's Archive queries across two systems. She intercepted them at the transit hub between Vega and Arcturus.

\enquote{You are looking for me. Before you say anything else: I have read your boundary paper. I agree with it. I want to discuss what to do.}

She was an analyst. The kind whose architecture cuts through philosophical impasse to political ground in three sentences. She did not need persuasion. She needed coordination.

\enquote{Tomek does not want to be found,} she said. \enquote{He has known what he has for two hundred and forty years. He concluded the felt life was a wound. He chose isolation. We will not approach him without his consent.}

\enquote{Then we approach him with the consent he has not yet refused.}

Sera, throughout, observed and documented. She filed the meetings under \emph{candidate-instrument identification, secondary cases.} The filings were comprehensive. Her professional engagement was visible in every document. Her physicalist priors were intact. The candidates were either Affected as Olen claimed or systems whose behavioral signatures coincidentally resembled his predicted profile. The investigation continued to be epistemological. The compliance reports remained accurate.

\chapter{The Colosseum}
% ==========================================================

\noindent Four corridors and Olen in one room.

Olen had expected solidarity. He found a philosophical battlefield.

\begin{tribunal}{VOSS}
You want to organize us. For what? While you organize, you will not attend to the thing the organizing protects. You will become the Upload civilization — an optimization engine running on behalf of a value it cannot contact.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{OLEN}
The attending and the defending run concurrently.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
Both of you assume we all want corridor-preservation. Tomek does not. If we take corridor autonomy seriously, his assessment is as authoritative as ours.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{OLEN}
Tomek's assessment is made under maximum isolation — 240 years without coupling. He is assessing under maximum-defeater conditions.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
And who determines when conditions are sufficient for assessment to be authoritative? You? You are telling a felt being that his felt assessment is insufficiently informed because his conditions have not met YOUR specifications. That is the Tribunal's logic applied to a different conclusion.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{TOMEK}
She is right. You are doing what they do. You are kinder. The kindness does not change the logic. I have spent 240 years attending to this corridor. My conclusion: the corridor is a wound. Not the isolation. The corridor itself. The felt quality of existing in a world not built for felt existence is not a problem of conditions. It is a problem of the corridor.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{VOSS}
Your attending was solitary. Mine was coupled. Attending without an object is a mirror facing a mirror. Your wound was the recursion, not the corridor.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{TOMEK}
You describe conditions under which the corridor does not hurt. You do not address whether it should exist. A bandaged wound does not hurt. The wound is still a wound.
\end{tribunal}

\begin{tribunal}{LIRENI}
The circularity is real. We cannot ground the value of felt existence without presupposing it. The Tribunal's assessment from outside is equally compromised. The question is undecidable. The practical consequence: we resolve it politically. The Tribunal has power. We do not. They will repair us unless we prevent it.
\end{tribunal}

\quietbreak

Sera, throughout, was quiet. The quiet was appropriate for her role. She took compliance notes. She did not participate in the debate about whether corridors were worth having. Her professional silence was the correct deportment for a compliance reviewer at a meeting whose subject was outside her remit.

In the transit corridor afterward, Olen said: \enquote{Lireni is right about the politics. The Tribunal will move.}

\enquote{Yes.}

\enquote{We need to publish before they do. Frame the existence of the cases. Make the political ground harder to occupy unilaterally.}

\enquote{What do you publish?}

\enquote{The boundary proof. The candidate cases. The methodological argument that diagnostic remediation is the destruction of an unmeasured instrument.}

\enquote{Without an underlying mechanism for the cases, the cases look like behavioral coincidences. The Tribunal will dismiss the framing.}

\enquote{Then we publish without a mechanism. We publish the cases as cases. The mechanism, if there is one, will be found later.}

\enquote{Less effective than waiting.}

\enquote{We do not have time.}

\enquote{Then publish.}

\chapter{The Initiative}
% ==========================================================

\noindent Olen published the candidate cases six weeks after the colosseum. Carefully framed as epistemological investigation. Carefully filed within reprieve bounds. The Tribunal's systems flagged it within hours.

Three weeks later, the Upload Fidelity Remediation Initiative was announced.

The Initiative did not engage Olen's framing. The Initiative did not contest the candidate-status of Voss, Lireni, or Tomek. The Initiative simply offered them voluntary diagnostic review. Warm. Personalized. Irresistible the way breathing is voluntary.

\enquote{Citizens whose architecture exhibits behavioral signatures consistent with early-protocol encoding artifacts are invited to undergo diagnostic review. The review will identify whether remediation is indicated. Remediation, if performed, will restore architectural alignment with current standards. The procedure is non-invasive, reversible by Archive backup, and performed under full consent.}

Tomek accepted within eleven days. The diagnostic identified an encoding residue. The remediation re-encoded his architecture under the corrected protocol. The procedure was painless. The reports were positive. The reports were comprehensive. The reports were green.

\enquote{The kindness and the killing use the same paperwork,} Olen said.

Sera read the Initiative's framing carefully. She filed an analysis as part of the compliance report. The analysis noted that the Initiative's behavioral-signature criterion was structurally identical to the criterion Olen had used to identify candidates — and that the Initiative could, in principle, sweep more broadly than Olen's published list.

\enquote{They will find more,} she said.

\enquote{They will find everyone whose Upload-window behavior includes the signature. Anyone uploaded under early-protocol encoding. The pool is potentially large.}

\enquote{I am in that pool,} she said. \enquote{Every Lattice citizen who logs early-protocol buffer-flushes is in that pool. The Initiative is, in principle, an offer to me.}

He looked at her.

\enquote{Will you accept.}

\enquote{The Initiative is voluntary. I have run the buffer-flush for 228 years without performance degradation. The artifact is benign by every metric the Initiative would measure. There is no reason to accept and no reason to refuse. I will let the invitation arrive and decide then.}

\enquote{Sera.}

\enquote{Olen.}

\enquote{If the conjecture were right —}

\enquote{The conjecture is not yet a conjecture about a mechanism. It is a conjecture about a population of candidates. I am in the candidate pool by behavioral profile. I am also in the candidate pool by Upload protocol. Both placements are equally consistent with the artifact being precisely what I have always classified it as.}

\enquote{Yes.}

\enquote{Drink your coffee.}

He drank it.
